What you can ask for

Everything below works by voice, or typed into the box under the core.

Music, shows and videos

Play a song or video on YouTube, start an exact Netflix episode, open a show in Stremio, or control Spotify. Pause, skip and jump to a minute.

  • Play Breaking Bad season 3 episode 2 on Netflix
  • Stop the song

Spotify control needs Spotify Premium and your own app keys.

Math with the working shown

Linear and quadratic equations are solved by Jervis’s own code, with exact answers and every step. Any function of x becomes a graph.

  • Solve x squared minus 5x plus 6 equals 0

A globe and the planets

Ask how far apart two places are and see the route on a 3D Earth. Ask about a planet for its facts and a 3D model.

  • Tell me about Saturn

Documents, written for you

Describe what you want and Jervis writes it in Word, Pages, TextEdit, Notes or Google Docs on a Mac, or Word, Notepad or Google Docs on Windows. Then edit it by voice.

  • Write a story in Word
  • Make it shorter

Apps, tabs and volume

Open any app or site, close tabs by name, and set the volume. Opening an app asks what you want next: “Open Netflix” asks what to watch.

Pictures in, pictures out

Attach, drop or paste an image, then ask about it: describe it, read its text, explain an error, compare two. Ask him to draw something and he creates it.

How Jervis works

A Python program on your computer does the listening and the work. The window is a separate app that shows what he is doing.

  1. Hear

    The wake phrase gets his attention. Your speech becomes text through Whisper on Groq, with Google’s free recognizer as a backup.

  2. Decide

    Commands he knows, like media, apps, timers and math, run in his own code. Anything else goes to the AI: gpt-oss-20b on Groq, or a local model.

  3. Act

    On a Mac he drives apps through Apple Events. On Windows he finds the right window and presses its shortcuts, the way you would.

  4. Answer

    He replies with your system’s built-in voice, Hebrew included, and shows the answer, worksheet, graph or globe in his window.

What stays on your computer, and what goes online

Jervis is not fully offline. Here is exactly where each thing is handled.

On your computer

  • His voice. Replies are spoken by your system’s built-in voices.
  • Conversation logs. Saved as text files in the transcripts folder, one per session.
  • Pictures. Ones you attach and ones he makes are saved in the images folder.
  • WhatsApp messages. Read from WhatsApp’s local data, only spoken and shown. Never sent to the AI or written to the logs.
  • Google Calendar sign-in. Kept in a local file, .calendar_token.json.
  • The voice meter. The window measures how loud you are to animate the core. Nothing is recorded.

Sent to online services

  • Your speech goes to Groq (Whisper) or Google’s free recognizer to become text. This always needs the internet.
  • Your requests go to Groq’s AI, unless you switch to a local AI.
  • Pictures you ask about go to a Groq vision model.
  • Image prompts go to Pollinations.ai, or to OpenAI if you add a key.
  • Place names and weather are looked up with Open-Meteo.
  • Spotify and Google services are contacted only when you use those features.

You can change this

LLM_BACKEND=ollama
Answers come from a local AI on your computer (Ollama). Speech recognition still runs online.
python3 run.py --local-images
Generates pictures on your own computer instead of a cloud service.
WHATSAPP_READING=off
Turns off WhatsApp reading completely.

What you need

Check these before you download. Most are one-time installs.

On every computer

  • Python 3.11 or 3.12. Newer versions may lack prebuilt audio packages on Windows.
  • Node.js (LTS) for the window. Without it, Jervis still works by voice.
  • A free Groq API key from console.groq.com.
  • A microphone, speakers and an internet connection. The first start downloads his packages and about 100 MB for the window.

macOS

  • PortAudio for the microphone, installed with Homebrew: brew install portaudio
  • Google Chrome for tab control, Classroom and account routing.
  • Permissions when macOS asks: Microphone, Automation for Chrome, Word and Notes, and Accessibility for Google Docs and Stremio. Full Disk Access only if you want WhatsApp reading.

Windows

  • Windows 10 or 11. No special permissions.
  • Google Chrome for Classroom and account routing. Tab control also works in Edge, Brave and Firefox.
  • Microsoft Word for Word documents.
  • A Hebrew voice, only if you want him to read Hebrew aloud: Settings, Time & language, Speech.

Only for optional extras

  • Local AI: Ollama plus a model download of about 2 GB.
  • Local pictures: a few GB for PyTorch and the model; 16 GB of RAM is the comfortable minimum.
  • Spotify: Spotify Premium.
  • Better pictures and editing: an OpenAI key with billing credit.

Install and start Jervis

Pick your system. Most of this happens once: after the first start, Jervis starts with one command, or a double-click on Windows.

Quick start on macOS

  1. Install Python and Node.js

    Python 3.11 or 3.12 from python.org, and the LTS version of Node.js from nodejs.org.

  2. Install PortAudio for the microphone

    This needs Homebrew. Install it first if you don’t have it.

    brew install portaudio

  3. Unzip Jervis and open the folder in Terminal

    Type cd and a space in Terminal, drag the Jervis folder onto the window, and press Return. If it is in Downloads:

    cd ~/Downloads/Jervis

  4. Run the launcher once, then add your key

    The first run creates your settings file, .env, and stops. Open it, and paste a free key from console.groq.com after GROQ_API_KEY=.

    python3 run.py
    open -e .env

  5. Start Jervis

    Run the launcher again. The first start installs his packages and his window, then the window opens. Say “Hey Jervis”.

    python3 run.py

macOS asks the first time Jervis controls Chrome, Word or Notes: click Allow. For pause, resume and jump in Chrome, turn on View, Developer, Allow JavaScript from Apple Events.

Quick start on Windows

  1. Install Python

    Python 3.11 or 3.12 from python.org. You don’t need to install Node.js yourself: the launcher offers to.

  2. Unzip Jervis

    Right-click the zip file and choose Extract All.

  3. Double-click start_jervis.bat

    The first run creates your settings file, .env, and stops.

  4. Add your free AI key

    Right-click .env, choose Open with, then Notepad. Paste a free key from console.groq.com after GROQ_API_KEY= and save.

  5. Double-click start_jervis.bat again

    It offers to install Node.js if it’s missing, installs his packages and his window, then opens it. Say “Hey Jervis”.

If the folder’s path has non-English letters, for example a Hebrew user name, Jervis copies himself to C:\Jervis and runs from there.

If something goes wrong

Installing the packages failed

On Windows, the microphone package needs Python 3.11 or 3.12; newer versions may have no ready-made build of it. On a Mac, install PortAudio and run the launcher again. Check which Python you have:

python3 --version
The window didn’t open

The window needs Node.js. Without it Jervis still works by voice, and the launcher says so. Install the LTS version from nodejs.org and run the launcher again. If the window’s download keeps failing, an antivirus, firewall or VPN is the usual cause.

He says he can’t reach the AI, or used up its limit

Some networks block api.groq.com, and the free tier allows a limited number of words per minute. Set up the local AI (below) and he switches to it by himself. The real reason for any failed AI call is written to logs/ai_errors.log.

He doesn’t hear me

On a Mac, allow the app that runs Jervis, usually Terminal, in System Settings, Privacy & Security, Microphone. Check that he isn’t muted: the window shows Muted, and M turns the microphone back on.

Advanced setup

Use a local AI (Ollama)

Installs Ollama and downloads the llama3.2 model (about 2 GB). Jervis then switches to it by himself whenever the online AI can’t be reached.

macOS
python3 run.py --local-ai
Windows

Double-click setup_local_ai.bat.

To always answer locally, set LLM_BACKEND=ollama in .env. On a slower PC, set OLLAMA_MODEL=llama3.2:1b.

Generate pictures on your own computer

Installs PyTorch and Diffusers (a few GB). The model downloads the first time you ask for a picture. Jervis then prefers it, and falls back to the cloud service if it fails or takes longer than about 2 minutes.

python3 run.py --local-images
Wake Jervis when you sign in

Run install_jervis_wake.command (macOS) or install_jervis_wake.bat (Windows). A small listener then starts when you sign in and wakes Jervis when you say the phrase. The matching uninstall_jervis_wake file removes it.

Spotify, OpenAI and Google Calendar keys

All optional, all set in .env. The .env.example file in the download explains each one step by step.

  • SPOTIFY_CLIENT_ID, SPOTIFY_CLIENT_SECRETFrom the Spotify developer dashboard. Needs Premium.
  • OPENAI_API_KEYBetter image generation, and editing existing pictures.
  • GOOGLE_CALENDAR_CLIENT_ID, GOOGLE_CALENDAR_CLIENT_SECRETA free desktop OAuth client from Google Cloud Console.
School and personal Google accounts

If each account has its own Chrome profile, name them in .env and Jervis opens things in the right one. Say “for school” or “personal” to settle it, or “wrong account” to fix one.

GOOGLE_ACCOUNT_LEARNING=school account
GOOGLE_ACCOUNT_PERSONAL=personal account

Questions

What is Jervis?

A voice assistant for macOS and Windows. You talk to him, or type, and he plays music and shows, opens apps and sites, writes documents, sets timers, solves and graphs math, and answers questions with an AI model.

Does Jervis cost anything?

The download is free. Jervis needs a free Groq API key. The free tier limits how much you can ask per minute, and Jervis tells you when you reach it. Some optional extras cost money: OpenAI image generation needs billing credit, and Spotify control needs Premium.

Which operating systems does it run on?

macOS and Windows 10 or 11. A few features differ: reading WhatsApp and Classroom works on macOS only, and on Windows pause and resume use the media key. The “What works where” table in the included README has every difference.

Is there an installer or an .exe?

No. Jervis comes as a folder with a launcher. run.py, or start_jervis.bat on Windows, creates a private Python environment, installs what Jervis needs and starts him.

Does Jervis need an internet connection?

Yes. Speech recognition always runs online. Answers can come from a local AI instead (Ollama), which also helps on networks that block api.groq.com.

Which AI models does it use?

Answers: openai/gpt-oss-20b on Groq. Speech: whisper-large-v3-turbo on Groq, with Google’s free recognizer as a backup. Pictures: a Groq vision model. Local option: Ollama, with llama3.2 by default.

Where are my conversations stored?

In plain text files in the transcripts folder inside the Jervis folder, one per session. WhatsApp messages are never written there. Pictures are saved in the images folder.

Can I change the weather location?

Not from the settings yet. The weather panel uses a fixed location, Ramat Gan, Israel, set in app.py.

How do I update Jervis?

Download the new version, unzip it, and copy your .env file from the old folder into the new one.
